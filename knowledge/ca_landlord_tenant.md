# California landlord-tenant: starting map

Where to look first. It is not a statement of current law. Agents must confirm
every rule against the current code text on leginfo.legislature.ca.gov before
relying on it, and check for local rent and eviction ordinances (Los Angeles,
San Francisco, Oakland, Berkeley, San Jose and others add stricter rules).

## Core statutes (Civil Code unless noted)
- **1940-1954.06**: hiring of real property (the main residential tenancy chapter).
- **1941.1, 1941**: habitability standards and the landlord's duty to keep the unit fit.
- **1942**: repair-and-deduct remedy (limits on amount and frequency).
- **1942.5**: retaliation protections.
- **1946.1**: notice to end a periodic tenancy (30/60 days).
- **1946.2**: Tenant Protection Act just-cause eviction rules, exemptions and relocation assistance.
- **1947.12**: statewide rent cap and its exemptions.
- **1950.5**: security deposits (amount limits, itemized statement and return deadline).
- **1954**: landlord entry (reasons and advance written notice).
- **Code of Civil Procedure 1161-1179a**: unlawful detainer (eviction) notices and procedure.
- **Government Code 12955**: housing discrimination (FEHA); includes source-of-income protection.

## Practical sources
- California Courts self-help, eviction pages and Judicial Council forms (UD-100, UD-105).
- California Department of Real Estate, "California Tenants" guide.
- Attorney General tenant rights page.

## Common matter types
Security deposit demand letters, repair requests and habitability complaints,
responding to 3-day notices, notices to end a tenancy, lease review, rent
increase compliance, entry disputes, and retaliation.

## Hard deadlines to flag every time
Unlawful detainer answer deadlines are very short (counted in court days after service).
Missing one can mean losing by default. Always tell the reviewer the date math and the source rule.
