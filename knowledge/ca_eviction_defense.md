# California eviction defense and roommate rent disputes: starting map

Where to look first. It is not a statement of current law. Agents must confirm
every rule, deadline and dollar limit against the current text on
leginfo.legislature.ca.gov or selfhelp.courts.ca.gov before relying on it.

## Eviction (unlawful detainer)
- **Code of Civil Procedure 1161**: grounds and notices, including the notice to pay rent or quit.
- **CCP 1162**: how a notice must be served.
- **CCP 1167**: time to respond after the summons and complaint are served (court days; check the current number).
- **CCP 1170, 1174.2**: the tenant's answer, and habitability as a defense to unpaid rent.
- **CCP 1179**: relief from forfeiture (asking the court to let the tenant stay after paying).
- **CCP 1161.2**: who can see eviction court records (masking).
- **Civil Code 1946.2**: Tenant Protection Act just cause; check whether the unit is covered.
- **Civil Code 1942.5**: retaliation.
- Judicial Council forms: UD-105 (Answer), UD-100 (Complaint), CP10.5 (Claim of Right to Possession
  for an occupant not named in the case), and fee waiver forms FW-001 and FW-003.

## Roommates and rent
- Who signed the lease decides who the landlord can hold responsible. Co-tenants who signed are
  usually each responsible for the full rent; a subtenant answers to the tenant who rented to them.
- Paying a roommate is not the same as paying the landlord. If a roommate kept the money, the landlord
  can still treat the rent as unpaid, and the tenant's claim is against the roommate.
- Protect the tenant now: pay the landlord directly with a traceable method, get receipts, tell the
  landlord in writing what happened, and save texts and payment app records (Venmo, Zelle, Cash App).

## Getting the money back from a roommate
- **CCP 116.110 and following**: small claims court. Check CCP 116.220 and 116.221 for the current limits.
- Usual steps: a written demand letter first, then form SC-100 (Plaintiff's Claim), then service (SC-104).
- **Penal Code 484, 487 and 496**: theft and receiving stolen property. A police report may fit when
  money given for a specific purpose was kept. Say whether the facts support it; never threaten
  criminal charges to get paid.
